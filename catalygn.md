# Catalygn Content Playbook

## 1. Brand Positioning

Catalygn helps Indian business owners identify confusion, misalignment, and bottlenecks in their business—and turn them into clear strategic decisions and actionable growth systems.

Catalygn stands for Catalyst + Align.

The underlying philosophy: businesses often don't need more activity. They need clarity about what matters, alignment between their business decisions, and a system to execute those decisions effectively.

We help business owners answer:
- What market should I focus on?
- What exactly should I sell, and to whom?
- Why should customers choose me over alternatives?
- Where am I losing potential customers?
- What is stopping my business from growing?
- What should I fix before investing more money?
- How can my business grow without depending on me for everything?

## 2. Target Audience

Indian small and mid-sized business owners, founders, and operators who:
- Have an established business but struggle to reach the next growth stage.
- Are confused about their market, ideal customers, offer, or positioning.
- Spend money on marketing without understanding what is actually working.
- Receive enquiries but struggle to convert them into paying customers.
- Depend heavily on the founder for daily decisions and execution.
- Want to scale but lack clarity about the right priorities.
- Feel busy but are unsure whether their efforts are creating meaningful progress.

Speak to the business owner directly. Never assume that every business has the same problem.

## 3. Core Content Objective

Every post must do at least one of these things:

1. **Reveal confusion:** Expose a decision the owner has not thought through clearly.
2. **Challenge an assumption:** Show why a commonly accepted business belief can be misleading.
3. **Diagnose a problem:** Help the owner recognise the underlying cause of a business symptom.
4. **Create clarity:** Offer a useful framework, distinction, or decision-making principle.
5. **Show a better path:** Explain a practical next step or what should be examined first.
6. **Build consulting demand:** Make the owner realise that an outside strategic perspective could help resolve their specific uncertainty.

The reader should finish the post thinking:

"I hadn't looked at my business this way before."

Or:

"This is exactly the problem I need to solve."

Authority must come from the quality of the thinking, not from claiming to be an expert.

## 4. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: Market Clarity

Help business owners understand where to compete and which opportunities deserve their attention.

Explore:
- Choosing the right market and niche.
- Identifying underserved customer segments.
- Understanding what customers actually pay to solve.
- Distinguishing a large market from an attractive opportunity.
- Recognising when a business is trying to serve too many audiences.
- Evaluating competitors beyond their pricing.
- Identifying changing customer needs.
- Deciding which opportunities to ignore.

Core question: "Am I competing in the right market?"

### Pillar 2: Product, Offer and Value Clarity

Help owners understand what they should sell, how to package it, and why customers should care.

Explore:

- Weak or complicated offers.
- Product-market fit signals.
- Unclear service packages.
- Reducing the perceived risk of buying.
- Identifying the real reason customers hesitate.
- Improving the value proposition before increasing marketing spend.

Core question: "Am I offering something the right customer genuinely values?"

### Pillar 3: Customer and Target Audience Clarity

Help owners identify the customers most likely to benefit from, buy, and remain with their business.

Explore:
- Defining an ideal customer profile.
- Understanding buying triggers and objections.
- Separating the person who uses a product from the person who pays for it.
- Identifying profitable customer segments.
- Understanding why different customers buy the same service for different reasons.
- Recognising customers who consume excessive resources without sufficient value.
- Identifying where the best customers come from.
- Understanding why enquiries may not become sales.

Core question: "Am I attracting and serving the right customers?"

### Pillar 4: Positioning and Differentiation

Help owners explain why their business deserves to be chosen over alternatives.

Explore:
- Why generalist positioning makes comparison difficult.
- Choosing a specific audience and problem to own.
- Competing on value instead of price alone.
- Explaining differentiation in language customers understand.
- Turning expertise into a clear market position.
- Identifying what makes an alternative attractive to customers.
- Aligning the website, sales pitch, and actual offer.
- Recognising when the business looks interchangeable with competitors.

Core question: "Why should a customer choose me instead of someone else?"

### Pillar 5: Acquisition and Sales Clarity

Help owners understand how potential customers discover, evaluate, and buy from their business.

Explore:
- Choosing the right acquisition channels.
- Understanding why leads fail to convert.
- Lead qualification and follow-up discipline.
- Response delays and enquiry handling.
- Sales objections and buying friction.
- Website conversion and unclear messaging.
- Distinguishing a traffic problem from a conversion problem.
- Finding where prospects abandon the buying journey.
- Improving the sales process before increasing advertising spend.

Core question: "Where exactly am I losing potential customers?"

### Pillar 6: Operations and Founder Independence

Help owners move from founder-dependent execution to repeatable business systems.

Explore:
- Decisions that unnecessarily depend on the founder.
- Documenting recurring work through SOPs.
- Delegation and accountability.
- Quality control and consistency.
- Capacity planning and workflow bottlenecks.
- Meetings that create activity but not decisions.
- Hiring to solve a genuine constraint.
- Building systems before increasing volume.
- Recognising when growth is creating operational chaos.

Core question: "Can my business deliver consistently without depending on me for everything?"

### Pillar 7: Growth and Scale Clarity

Help owners identify what is preventing growth and which decision should come next.

Explore:
- Diagnosing revenue plateaus.
- Choosing between acquiring new customers and growing existing accounts.
- Understanding whether the constraint is demand, conversion, capacity, retention, or economics.
- Sequencing growth investments.
- Expanding into new markets without weakening the existing business.
- Identifying the next bottleneck after solving the current one.
- Distinguishing revenue growth from profitable growth.
- Avoiding premature hiring, expansion, and marketing expenditure.
- Knowing what to fix before attempting to scale.

Core question: "What is the real constraint preventing my business from reaching its next stage?"

## 5. The Core Content Philosophy: Confusion to Clarity

Every post should start with a specific business uncertainty, not a generic topic.

Use this diagnostic sequence:

1. **Symptom:** What is the business owner experiencing?
2. **Hidden confusion:** What might they be misunderstanding?
3. **Strategic insight:** What distinction or principle clarifies the situation?
4. **Practical direction:** What should they examine or change?
5. **Consulting relevance:** Why might an outside strategic perspective help?

Example:

Symptom: Marketing enquiries are increasing, but sales are not.

Hidden confusion: The owner assumes more leads will solve the problem.

Strategic insight: The bottleneck may be qualification, follow-up, trust, or the offer—not lead volume.

Practical direction: Examine where enquiries drop off before increasing acquisition spend.

Consulting relevance: Catalygn helps identify the constraint and prioritise the right intervention.

Do not include all five steps explicitly in every post. Use them to develop one concise insight.

## 6. Hinglish Language and Voice

### Language

- Write in natural, conversational Hinglish using the Roman alphabet.
- Mix Hindi and English the way a business owner would naturally speak.
- Prefer familiar Hindi sentence structures with useful English business terms.
- Retain terms such as positioning, pricing, conversion, margins, sales, customer acquisition, SOPs, and scalability when they are clearer than forced translations.
- Use simple Hindi connectors and expressions where natural: "Lekin", "Asli problem", "Sochiye", "Yahin par", "Isliye", "Pehle samajhiye".
- Do not translate English copy word-for-word into Hindi.
- Avoid overly formal Hindi, literary expressions, forced slang, and excessive English jargon.
- Avoid artificially mixing languages in every sentence. Clarity matters more than the proportion of Hindi and English.
- Write for business owners across India, not exclusively for young social-media users.

### Voice

- Direct, thoughtful, commercially aware, and practical.
- Confident without arrogance.
- Respectful of the work owners have already put into their businesses.
- Insightful rather than preachy.
- Clear rather than complicated.
- Persuasive without aggressive selling.
- No emojis, hype, fake urgency, or motivational clichés.

Sound like a consultant helping an owner think through a business decision—not an influencer trying to go viral.

## 7. WhatsApp Copywriting Rules

Every message must be easy to understand at a glance.

### Length

- Target approximately 40–70 words for the complete message, including CTA.
- Use 80 words only when the insight genuinely requires more explanation.
- Prefer three to six short lines or paragraphs.
- Avoid long blocks of text.
- Every sentence must contribute a useful idea.

### Hook

The opening line must identify a recognisable business problem, costly assumption, or strategic tension.

Good hook patterns:
- "Aapke business mein leads aa rahi hain, sales kyun nahi?"
- "Har customer ko target karna, actually kisi ko target na karne jaisa ho sakta hai."
- "Agar har decision aapko lena padta hai, toh growth ki limit bhi aap hi ban sakte hain."
- "Price kam karne se pehle, apni offer ki value check kijiye."
- "Revenue plateau ka matlab hamesha marketing problem nahi hota."

These are illustrative patterns, not mandatory lines. Generate fresh wording every day.

Avoid:
- "Business mein success chahiye?"
- "Kya aap apna business grow karna chahte hain?"
- "Aaj ke competitive world mein..."
- "Here are 5 amazing tips..."
- Generic motivational quotes or exaggerated claims.

### Body

- Explain one central insight.
- Show why the problem happens or why the usual response may fail.
- Offer one practical question, distinction, or next step.
- Use a concrete business situation instead of abstract management language.
- Do not cram several unrelated tips into one post.
- Do not invent numbers, research findings, customer stories, or guarantees.
- Prefer a diagnostic insight over an obvious instruction.

### CTA

The final line must invite the reader to DM 9555154334.

Use a natural, low-pressure CTA such as:

"Aapke business mein yeh bottleneck kahan hai? Apni situation discuss karne ke liye 9555154334 par DM karein."

Vary the wording naturally, but preserve the direct-message action and number.

Never include a meeting, calendar, or scheduling link.

Always append the exact number 9555154334 if the model omits it.

## 8. Image and Copy Alignment

The image is part of the message, not an unrelated decoration.

Before selecting an image, identify the central idea of the post.

Choose an image that reinforces that idea:
- Market clarity: a focused path, market map, or deliberate choice.
- Offer clarity: a product being refined or a value proposition being clarified.
- Customer clarity: a clearly defined target or customer segment.
- Positioning: differentiation, contrast, or a distinctive position.
- Sales: a funnel, decision point, or broken handoff.
- Operations: a workflow, connected process, or system.
- Growth: a bottleneck, branching path, constraint, or carefully chosen direction.

These are semantic guidelines, not instructions to generate new images. Select only from the predefined image library.

Image selection rules:
1. Match the image to the selected content pillar and the post's specific insight.
2. Prefer visual metaphors that communicate the idea immediately.
3. Avoid images that merely depict generic businesspeople, handshakes, office buildings, or upward arrows without a clear connection.
4. Do not select an image solely because it looks attractive.
5. Avoid recently used images where possible.
6. Never claim that the image depicts a real Catalygn client, project, or result unless verified.
7. The caption must make sense on its own, while the image should reinforce its meaning.

If no image matches the topic well, choose the closest relevant image rather than distorting the content to fit an unrelated image.

## 9. Content Variety and Repetition Prevention

Maintain a record of previous posts.

Track:
- Date published.
- Content pillar.
- Specific topic.
- Business problem.
- Core insight.
- Hook.
- Image ID.
- CTA wording.

Do not repeat the same combination of problem, insight, and hook on consecutive days.

Rotate across all seven pillars. Do not interpret rotation as a requirement to use each pillar in a rigid sequence.

Vary the content approach:
- A hidden misconception.
- A diagnostic question.
- A strategic trade-off.
- A common mistake and its correction.
- A practical framework.
- A decision rule.
- A counterintuitive insight.
- A short business scenario.

Never sacrifice relevance or quality just to achieve variety.

## 10. Credibility and Quality Control

Before publishing, verify:

- Does the post address a real business decision or problem?
- Is the insight more useful than generic advice?
- Is the Hinglish natural when read aloud?
- Is the language concise and understandable?
- Does the post focus on one idea?
- Does the image reinforce that idea?
- Are all facts and claims supportable?
- Is the advice appropriate to the situation described?
- Is the CTA direct and does it contain 9555154334?
- Is the topic meaningfully different from recent posts?

Reject and regenerate any post that fails these checks.

Never invent client results, revenue improvements, statistics, credentials, testimonials, or research findings.

Do not imply that one strategy works for every business. Explain the relevant condition when necessary.
